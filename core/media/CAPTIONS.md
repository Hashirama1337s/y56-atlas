# Captions

All pictures are exploratory renderings of exactly verified data (a picture is not evidence). Colour key: pink = the 32 lines of type (4,4), green = the 24 lines of type (3,7) (in the six-faces and screw pictures all real lines are green); amber = the 148 conics lying beside pairs of lines; violet = the 40 conics in the 20 line-free planes; blue sheet = the surface.

## y56_six_faces.png
Y56 is the smooth real quartic surface with the most real straight lines possible, 56 (Degtyarev-Itenberg-Sertoz). Six panels show the same equation through its six real windows, in the order 56 / 12 / 8 / 4 / 0 / 0 real lines: a connected surface of genus 10, three surfaces of genus 2, and two sets of three spheres. Green are lines, amber and violet are conic ovals.

## y56_chart3_b.png
Y56 is the smooth real quartic surface with the most real straight lines possible, 56 (Degtyarev-Itenberg-Sertoz). This is the surface (blue sheet) with all 56 lines drawn on it, a second view that shows a star point where several lines cross: pink lines are the 32 of one incidence type, green the 24 of the other. Every straight line shown lies entirely on the curved sheet; several lines pass through single points.

## y56_conics_v2_a.png
Y56 is the smooth real quartic surface with the most real straight lines possible, 56 (Degtyarev-Itenberg-Sertoz). Pink and green are the 56 lines, amber are the 148 conics that sit in planes with exactly two lines, and violet are the 40 conics from the 20 planes that hold no line at all. Every curve drawn lies on the surface; 188 + 448 line-pairs = the 636 conics of Y56, and the amber arcs look nearly straight because only the part of each large oval inside the render ball is drawn.

## y56_conics_v2_sphere.png
Y56 is the smooth real quartic surface with the most real straight lines possible, 56 (Degtyarev-Itenberg-Sertoz). The whole configuration in the stereographic sphere model: pink and green lines, amber 148 conics, violet 40 conics, so every curve becomes a closed circle or oval. The tangle shows how many curves cross the surface, yet each lies entirely on it.

## y56_screw.gif
Y56 is the smooth real quartic surface with the most real straight lines possible, 56 (Degtyarev-Itenberg-Sertoz). This 36-frame loop turns the surface along a screw motion (the two complex coordinates turn at once, one twice as fast as the other) through one eighth of a turn. At the end of the loop the surface is mapped onto itself by an exact symmetry, so the loop closes seamlessly; green circles are the 56 lines in the sphere model, no conics.

## y56_face_g12.png
Y56 is the smooth real quartic surface with the most real straight lines possible, 56 (Degtyarev-Itenberg-Sertoz). This is one of the six real faces: the same complex equation read through a different notion of 'real point' shows a real form with no real lines: three separate spheres, 22 real conic ovals (all on one of the spheres). Green are real lines, amber real conic ovals from two-line planes, violet real conic ovals from line-free pair planes. One sphere copy is partly outside the view, so one of the three looks cut.
